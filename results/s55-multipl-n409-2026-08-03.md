# §5.5 at n=409 on a standard benchmark: the primary claim replicates and the margin widens — 2026-08-03

Every prior experiment in this directory ran on the project's own 64 hand-written
problems. The §5.5(1) bar is **n ≥ 300 on a standard benchmark**, and the gap between
"demonstrated" and "validated" was exactly that. This run closes the sample-size and
benchmark-standardness parts of it: **488 MultiPL-E Go problems** (HumanEval-Go +
MBPP-Go, `~/mple-bench`, built by `examples/bench/multipl/`), **409 usable after
oracle-soundness exclusions**.

**The primary comparative claim (§5.5(3)) replicates, and the margin is far wider than
at n=64.**

| oracle | lowest certifiable α (δ=0.10) | empirical risk | realized risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **execution** | **0.084** | 0.0538 | 0.0538 |
| **judge** | **0.226** | 0.1883 | 0.0587 |

The margin over the judge widens monotonically with n, in both the ratio and the
absolute gap:

| n | execution α | judge α | ratio | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 28 | 0.270 | 0.320 | 1.19× | 0.050 |
| 64 | 0.190 | 0.300 | 1.58× | 0.110 |
| **409** | **0.084** | **0.226** | **2.69×** | **0.142** |

Both n=409 boundaries are pinned to ±0.001 by an offline α sweep over the saved records
(`-from-records`, free), each arm swept against its **own** records — not read off a
single certificate. The n=28 and n=64 rows are from experiments 7 and 8.

**Execution was sound again, and this is now a much stronger statement than before:
1096/1096 tier observations agree with ground truth — 0 over-accepts and 0
over-rejects.** β = 0 held at 16× the previous sample size.

## Setup

- `examples/bench/config.go-specialist-211.json`: Maverick (tier 0, $0.24/$0.97),
  Sonnet 4.5 (tier 1), Opus 4.5 (tier 2), fan-out **2:1:1**. Oracle/judge model
  `claude-sonnet-4-6`, distinct from every tier — **0 records contaminated**
  (invariant #3 held).
- `-refs ~/mple-bench/refs -pin-api -compare -alpha 0.05 -delta 0.10`, n = 488.
  Records `results/s55-fixed.records.{execution,judge}.json`.
- **Live spend $8.15** ($0.0167/problem), 4h20m, completed 488/488 with no external
  kill.
- **This run required a bug fix mid-flight** — see "What this run is a re-run of".

## The judge's error budget is 14:1 over-rejection, and η_fa is finally more than one point

Both arms ruled on the **identical** candidate stream (`ProfilePaired`), so every
difference below is the oracle rather than sampling.

```
judge vs execution ground truth, 1096 comparable tier observations
  agree            930
  over-ACCEPT       11   (η_fa — the dangerous direction, invisible to its certificate)
  over-REJECT      155   (β — costs escalations, not risk)
```

Over-rejection outnumbers over-acceptance **14:1**, which reproduces the mechanism
finding from n=64: *the judge loses the certification race mainly by being wrong in the
safe direction.* Its realized risk (0.0587) is **lower** than its empirical risk
(0.1883) — it certifies against an inflated number because it rejects correct code, and
that inflation is what forces its α up to 0.226.

**But the dangerous direction is now measurable rather than anecdotal.** Across the
whole study before this run, η_fa > 0 had been observed **exactly once** (the pilot's
scar-free race). Here there are **11 over-acceptances**, concentrated in the cheap tier:

| tier | over-accept | over-reject |
|---|---|---|
| small | 8 | 46 |
| mid | 2 | 55 |
| large | 1 | 54 |

That gradient is what §3.1 predicts and what the certificate cannot see: the judge's
false-acceptance rate is highest exactly where the code is worst.

**Limitation, stated rather than papered over: these 11 are not classified.** The
records store per-tier verdicts, not candidate source (`Record.tiers` has
`correct`/`true_correct`/`score`/`cost_usd` and no program text), so the defect class
behind each over-acceptance **cannot be recovered post hoc**. The paper's claim is
specifically that the judge's blind spot is *reading-invisible* defects; this run
establishes that η_fa > 0 at a measurable rate and that it concentrates in the cheap
tier, but it does **not** confirm the mechanism. Retaining accepted-candidate source for
disagreeing observations is the change that would let a future run close this, and it
costs disk rather than money.

## Cost: the cascade wins, but not at the tightest certifiable α

Baselines over the **409 usable** problems (ground-truth risk, mean cost/query):

| policy | risk | cost/query |
|---|---|---|
| always-cheapest (d) | 0.2298 | $0.00035 |
| always-frontier (a) | 0.0587 | $0.00616 |

The certified cascade (b) depends on α, and there is a sharp transition:

| α | thresholds | cascade cost/query | vs frontier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.084–0.105 | `[1, 1]` | $0.01008 | **1.6× pricier** |
| 0.11–0.25 | `[0.1, 1]` | $0.00290 | **2.2× cheaper** |

This is the **same tension experiments 12–17 found at n=64, reproduced at n=409**: the
tightest certifiable α forces `[1, 1]`, full escalation, and a cascade that costs more
than simply always calling the frontier model. The cost win requires α ≥ 0.11. The
transition is at **α = 0.11** (`[1,1]` at 0.105, `[0.1,1]` at 0.11).

So the honest summary of the value proposition is unchanged by scale: *the cascade beats
always-frontier by ~2.2× at matched risk when the risk target permits the cheap tier to
be accepted, and is pricier than frontier when it does not.* What n=409 adds is that
this is a structural property, not a small-sample artifact.

**α = 0.05 still does not certify** — the lowest is 0.084, and the binding constraint is
now visible: empirical risk 0.0538 over 409 problems is a genuine model-accuracy floor,
not oracle noise (execution's oracle agreed with ground truth 1096/1096). At n=64 the
equivalent floor was 0/52 errors and α=0.05 *did* certify; at n=409 the model makes real
mistakes and the certificate correctly refuses to go below them. **This is the more
trustworthy of the two results.**

## The exclusions are load-bearing, and one number proves it

79 of 488 problems were excluded as `OracleUnsound` (16.2%); 0 contaminated, 0 cache
hits (arm zero is cold by construction — invariant #8).

```
                        n     frontier risk   cheapest risk
all records            488       0.1762          0.3156
usable (kept)          409       0.0587          0.2298
the 79 excluded         79       0.7848          0.7595
```

**The excluded problems have frontier risk 0.785.** They are overwhelmingly problems
where the *generated* suite refutes even Opus — which is precisely what the `-refs`
soundness gate exists to detect (invariant #4). Reporting risk over all 488 would charge
the router 0.1762 for what are mostly oracle defects.

This also flags a discrepancy a reader will otherwise hit: `calibrate -baselines` prints
its baseline table over **all** records (0.1762 / 0.3156) while `results/analyze_s55.py`
reports over the **usable** 409 (0.0587 / 0.2298). Both are correct for their
denominator. The usable figure is the one that answers "how risky is this router";
the all-records figure includes problems where being correct and passing the oracle are
different things. **Quote the denominator with the number.**

## What this run is a re-run of, and the fix it validates

The first attempt at this experiment completed 487/488 for **$7.82** and is preserved
as `results/s55-n470.records.{execution,judge}.json` (committed, because the paired
comparison below is not reproducible without it and the code that produced it is now
fixed — regenerating would require a revert). It is superseded because a diagnostic
pattern in its own records
exposed a bug in `prompt.ExtractAPI` (**PR #47**, merged): the API stub kept the source's
import block verbatim after blanking every function body, so any import used *only* by a
body became an **unused import — a compile error in Go**. The stub is pasted into the
spec prompt under "use exactly this API", so it propagated into `spec.API`, failed
`testsCompileAgainstOwnAPI`, and made `validateOracle` flag a **sound** oracle as
`OracleUnsound`. Measured A/B on the benchmark: **133/470 → 0/470**.

The failure direction is **conservative** — a non-compiling stub can only cause a *false*
exclusion, never mask a real one — so the pre-fix certificate was not invalid. What it
corrupted was `n` and the exclusion rate.

**Paired effect of the fix, on the 371 ids both runs cover:**

| | pre-fix | fixed | recovered | new |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `oracle_unsound` | 93 (25.1%) | 63 (17.0%) | 47 | **17** |
| all-tier-score-0 | 66 (17.8%) | 35 (9.4%) | 40 | **9** |

Net exclusions fall ~8 points, in the predicted direction. **But there are 17 new
exclusions and 9 new all-zero rows**, and they are *not* evidence against the fix: the
re-run resamples at temperature > 0, so records churn in both directions independently
of any code change. Experiment 19 measured exactly this instability — two draws of the
same configuration agreed on only 159/192 rows. The fix's *mechanism* is one-directional;
the *observed record set* is not. An early read of the first 19 ids showed 0 new
exclusions and would have supported a cleaner claim than the data does at n=371.

## What this closes and what it does not

**Closes:**
- §5.5(1)'s **n ≥ 300 on a standard benchmark** — n=409 usable, HumanEval-Go/MBPP-Go.
- §5.5(3)'s comparative claim at that scale, with a **2.7× α gap** (0.084 vs 0.226).
- Execution soundness at scale: **1096/1096**, β=0.
- η_fa **> 0 at a measurable rate** (11/1096) for the first time, with a cheap-tier
  gradient.

**Does not close:**
- **The η_fa mechanism.** 11 events, defect classes unrecoverable from the records (see
  above). "The judge's blind spot is reading-invisible defects" remains argued.
- **α = 0.05.** Lowest certifiable is 0.084, floored by genuine model accuracy (0.0538).
- **The deployable-α cost win.** Certifiable α and the cost win are still disjoint here
  (`[1,1]` below α=0.11), reproducing the n=64 tension at 6× the scale.
- **Arm (e), self-consistency.** Unimplemented; not run here.
- **The race rung.** MultiPL-E Go contains **0 concurrency problems** (verified: no
  reference solution in `~/mple-bench/refs/*/solution.go` contains `go func`, `chan`,
  `sync.`, or `goroutine`, and no problem statement mentions concurrency), so the
  `-race` stage — the rung that caught the study's only confirmed judge
  over-acceptance — was never exercised. The AST predicate that gates it never fired. A benchmark that standardises on single-function puzzles cannot
  test the ladder's most expensive stage. This is a real coverage gap and a reason the
  64-problem hand-written set is not obsolete.
- **18/488 problems have no reference** and therefore no soundness gate (documented in
  `examples/bench/multipl/README.md`); they are inside the 488 but carry no `-refs`
  check.
- **§5.4's external-validity restriction.** Still single-file, stdlib-only Go.

## Reproducing

```bash
# the run itself (costs ~$8, ~4h)
go-cascade calibrate -provider=bedrock \
  -config examples/bench/config.go-specialist-211.json \
  -bench ~/mple-bench/problems.jsonl -refs ~/mple-bench/refs \
  -pin-api -compare -alpha 0.05 -delta 0.10 \
  -records results/s55-fixed.records.json -o results/s55-fixed.cert.json

# every figure above, from the saved records, free
python3 results/analyze_s55.py results/s55-fixed.records

# the pre-fix run, for comparison (same command, before PR #47)
python3 results/analyze_s55.py results/s55-n470.records

# the α boundaries, free. Each arm is swept against its OWN records — the judge's
# boundary comes from the judge arm, not from replaying execution records at a
# different α, or the comparison would not be between oracles at all.
for arm in execution judge; do
  for a in 0.083 0.084 0.224 0.226; do
    go-cascade calibrate -from-records results/s55-fixed.records.$arm.json \
      -alpha $a -delta 0.10 -config examples/bench/config.go-specialist-211.json \
      -o /tmp/c.json | grep -E "^  valid|^  thresholds"
  done
done
# execution: false at 0.083, true at 0.084.  judge: false at 0.224, true at 0.226.
```
