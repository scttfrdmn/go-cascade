# 5:1:1 fan-out — deployable α=0.05 AND the cost win, together — 2026-07-30

Experiment 12 ([`pinned-n64-complete`](pinned-n64-complete-2026-07-30.md)) reached
a deployable α=0.05 certificate but found the cost win *inverted* there: the
certified thresholds `[1,1]` forced full escalation, so the cascade cost more than
always-frontier. The [tension diagnosis](tension-diagnosis-2026-07-30.md) localised
that to the cheap tier's 2-sample Wilson ceiling (0.425): a lone flaky-but-unanimous
wrong answer (`scale_chunk`) was score-indistinguishable from correct unanimous
answers, so rejecting it collapsed τ0 to 1.0. The predicted fix: **raise the tier-0
fan-out** to (a) lift the unanimity ceiling, giving the threshold headroom, and
(b) split flaky clusters. This run tests it — tier 0 at **5 samples** (5:1:1),
everything else identical to the 2:1:1 config.

## Result — the tension is resolved

```
arm         valid  cert-α  emp-risk  real-risk  verdict
execution   true   0.050   0.0000    0.0000     certified risk holds
judge       false  0.050   0.0943    0.0189     could not certify

certificate: thresholds [0.4 1], n=53 clean (11 oracle-unsound excluded)
```

Both success criteria from the diagnosis are met **simultaneously** — the thing
that was impossible at 2:1:1:

| | 2:1:1 (exp 12) | **5:1:1 (this run)** |
|---|---|---|
| α=0.05 certifies? | yes | **yes** |
| tier-0 threshold τ0 | **1.0** (never-accept) | **0.4** (cheap tier accepted) |
| cascade vs frontier cost | 0.61× (**pricier**) | **2.13× cheaper** |
| genuine model errors (clean) | 0/52 | **0/53** |
| gate coverage | 100% (0 inconclusive) | 100% (0 inconclusive) |

Clean-set baselines (n=53, τ=[0.4, 1], ground truth):

| policy | risk | mean cost/query |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| always-cheapest | 0.094 | $0.00135 |
| **cascade** | **0.000** | **$0.00394** |
| always-frontier | 0.000 | $0.00841 |

So at a deployable α=0.05 the cascade is **2.13× cheaper than always-frontier at
zero risk** — deployable certification and the cost win, at the same operating
point. Live spend ≈ $2.3 recorded + spec/pin (SIGTERM'd at 59/64; `-resume`
finished the tail, no data lost — the resume fix earning its keep for the third
time this session).

## The mechanism — real headroom, with a fresh-draw confound

At 2 samples every unanimous answer scored 0.425 (the Wilson ceiling), leaving no
gap between "confidently right" and "confidently wrong." At **5 samples the score
distribution spreads out and gains headroom**:

```
tier0 score histogram (clean, 5 samples): {0: 4, 0.046: 2, 0.143: 2, 0.435: 4, 0.649: 41}
```

Unanimous (5/5) answers now score **0.649**, and τ0=0.4 sits well below that
ceiling while still admitting the 45 clusters (41 unanimous + 4 at 0.435) — **none
of which were oracle-wrong**. That headroom is structural: a threshold *can* now be
placed to accept genuine agreement and reject weak agreement, which was impossible
when the ceiling itself was 0.425.

**The honest confound:** `scale_chunk` — the specific 2:1:1 blocker — this run drew
**correct** (score 0.435, i.e. 4/5 agreement, one dissenting sample). So the result
combines two effects that this single run cannot separate:

1. **Structural headroom** from 5 samples (unanimity ceiling 0.425 → 0.649) —
   demonstrated, draw-independent.
2. **A fresh draw** that happened to put `scale_chunk` correct — luck, not fan-out.

The 4/5 (not 5/5) agreement on `scale_chunk` is *consistent* with the flaky-error
hypothesis (a higher fan-out surfaces the instability as a split cluster), but it is
one data point, not proof — the wrong answer did not recur this draw, so we did not
directly observe a flaky *wrong* cluster being split below τ0. What is
draw-independent and solid is the **headroom**: the 5-sample ceiling gives the
calibrator room to certify α=0.05 with cheap acceptance, where the 2-sample ceiling
did not.

## What is established

1. **Deployable α=0.05 and the cost win are jointly achievable at 5:1:1.** τ0=0.4
   (cheap tier accepted), cascade 2.13× cheaper than always-frontier at 0 risk,
   α=0.05 certified. The mutual-exclusivity of experiment 12 was a property of the
   2:1:1 fan-out, not of the method.
2. **A higher tier-0 fan-out buys threshold headroom** — the unanimity ceiling rose
   0.425 → 0.649, giving the certificate a threshold below the ceiling to place.
   This is the structural, draw-independent lever.
3. **The cheap tier remains safe.** 0 tier-0 acceptance-risk events again; the 45
   admitted clusters are all oracle-correct. Accepting Maverick at τ0=0.4 added no
   ground-truth risk.

## Honest limitations

- **One draw; the specific blocker resolved partly by luck.** `scale_chunk` drew
  correct this run, so the clean separation of "fan-out fixed it" from "the draw
  fixed it" is not available. The headroom is structural; the disappearance of *the*
  blocker is confounded. A repeat draw (or a seeded-flaky-error test) would settle
  which dominates.
- **Cross-run risk is not comparable** (fresh candidates each run). The robust
  claims are the *within-run* cost comparison and the *threshold* (τ0<1.0), not the
  risk magnitude.
- **5 samples raises tier-0 cost.** Maverick is cheap enough that 5:1:1 still beats
  frontier 2.13×, but the fan-out is a cost knob: too high and the cheap tier's
  aggregate cost erodes the win (the 5:2:1 lesson from experiment 9). 5:1:1 is not
  claimed optimal.
- **Same single benchmark, single judge model, small n.** Directions robust,
  magnitudes are point estimates.
