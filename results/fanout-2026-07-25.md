# Fan-out test: does flattening the sample counts fix the cascade's cost? — 2026-07-25

The cost baseline (`cost-baseline-2026-07-25.md`) found always-frontier beat the
cascade, and traced it to the default **5:2:1** sample fan-out: the cheap tier
draws 5 candidates, so 5 Haiku calls ≈ 1 Opus call and the cheap tier isn't cheap
per problem. The predicted fix was a flatter fan-out. This tests it: re-profile
the combined n=64 benchmark at **1:1:1** (one sample per tier), execution oracle,
then rerun the cost baseline.

## Result

Cost baseline at each fan-out (n=64, execution oracle, ground-truth risk):

| policy          | 5:2:1 risk | 5:2:1 cost | 1:1:1 risk | 1:1:1 cost |
|-----------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| always-cheapest | 0.109     | $0.00784  | 0.141     | $0.00158  |
| cascade         | 0.094     | $0.00928  | 0.125     | $0.00289  |
| always-frontier | 0.094     | $0.00789  | 0.141     | $0.00796  |

**At 1:1:1 the cascade wins on cost: $0.00289 vs always-frontier's $0.00796 —
2.75× cheaper — at no worse risk (0.125 vs 0.141).** The predicted fix works.

## What is clean here, and what is not

**Clean (structural, not noise): the cost mechanism.** Flattening the fan-out
dropped the cheap tier's per-query cost from $0.00784 to $0.00158 (5× fewer
samples), so problems that stop at the cheap tier are now genuinely cheap, and
the cascade — which keeps most queries there and escalates only ~30–40% — comes
in far under always-frontier. Always-frontier's cost barely moved ($0.00789 →
$0.00796) because it never used the cheap tier. This is the fan-out effect,
confirmed and structural.

**Not clean (sampling noise): the risk column across runs.** Always-frontier is
the *same* Opus model in both runs, yet its risk changed 0.094 → 0.141. That is
pure sampling variance — each run draws its own single (or few) Opus candidates,
and n=64 is small. So **do not compare risk across the two runs.** Within the
1:1:1 run all three policies saw the same draws, so the *within-run* comparison
(cascade 0.125 ≤ frontier 0.141) is fair; but with 1 sample the numbers are noisy
and the risk win is weak. The robust claim is the **cost** win, not the risk win.

## Honest bottom line

- **The cascade's cost disadvantage was a tuning artifact, not fundamental.** At
  the default 5:2:1 it loses to always-frontier; at 1:1:1 it wins on cost by
  ~2.75× at comparable risk. The earlier "always-frontier wins" result stands for
  the *default config*, and this shows it is fixable by config alone.
- **Caveat — the fan-out is not free.** Multiple samples exist for a reason:
  behavioural clustering needs >1 candidate to earn a confident routing score
  (the Wilson lower bound). At 1 sample the cluster score is degenerate, so the
  cascade's *escalation decisions* are less well-informed than at 5:2:1. This run
  optimised cost at the expense of routing signal. The real sweet spot is likely
  intermediate (e.g. 2:1:1 or 3:2:1), trading a little sampling cost for enough
  signal — an open tuning question.
- **This does not overturn the honest negative.** "Cheaper at equal correctness"
  is now *demonstrated for a 1:1:1 config* on this benchmark, but with weak risk
  resolution. A confident claim needs larger n and a fan-out that preserves
  routing signal. What is solid: the fan-out is the dominant cost lever, exactly
  as the baseline predicted.

## Ties to the specialist-model idea

This sharpens why a cheap, accurate Go-specialist bottom tier is attractive: it
would make tier 0 cheap *without* dropping to 1 sample (preserving cluster
signal), and more accurate (fewer escalations, lower risk floor). The fan-out
result shows tier-0 cost is the lever; a specialist is a cleaner way to pull it
than starving the sample count.

## Reproduce

```bash
# Re-profile at 1:1:1 (live, ~$4) with a config overriding samples to 1 per tier,
# then score the baseline offline:
go-cascade calibrate --provider=bedrock --config flat-config.json \
  -bench examples/bench/combined.jsonl --oracle=execution -records flat.json
go-cascade calibrate -from-records flat.json -alpha 0.15 -delta 0.10 -baselines -o /tmp/c.json
```

Live spend this run: ~$4. Session total: roughly $33–37.
