# Pilot run — 2026-07-25

First live evaluation of go-cascade against Bedrock. 28-problem pilot benchmark
(`examples/bench/problems.jsonl`), both oracles, α=0.10, δ=0.10.

## Setup

- **Tiers:** `us.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5` (small, 5 samples),
  `claude-sonnet-4-5` (mid, 2), `claude-opus-4-5` (large, 1).
- **Oracle / judge model:** `us.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-6` — chosen distinct
  from every tier so the contamination guard (invariant #3) does not exclude
  every record.
- **Benchmark:** 28 problems, 8 concurrency, all with reference implementations
  validated by execution (`examples/bench/validate.sh`, 28/28 pass).
- Command: `calibrate --provider=bedrock --compare`, both arms, 28 problems each.
  Zero skips or errors.

## Result

| arm       | valid | cert-α | empirical risk | realized risk | verdict          |
|-----------|-------|--------|----------------|---------------|------------------|
| execution | false | 0.100  | 0.1429         | 0.1429        | could not certify |
| judge     | false | 0.100  | 0.0714         | 0.0714        | could not certify |

## What this establishes

1. **The executable oracle is sound in practice.** Realized risk equals
   empirical risk exactly for the execution arm (β=0 holds): what it accepts on
   the visible partition, it delivers on the hidden one, up to the oracle gap.
   4 of 28 accepted solutions were in fact wrong — the first live risk
   measurement this project has.
2. **Neither arm certifies at α=0.10**, exactly as the sample-size math predicts:
   with 2–4 observed errors at n=28, no threshold clears δ=0.10. This is the
   binding constraint the paper calls out (need n≥22 even at zero errors, and
   these arms are not at zero errors).

## What this does NOT establish (honest caveats)

- **The mock's headline effect did not reproduce.** In the mock, the judge arm
  showed realized risk (0.22) far above its empirical risk (0.00) — the §3.1
  noise floor. Here the judge arm's realized risk *matched* its empirical and
  came in *below* the execution arm. At this scale the §3.1 comparative claim is
  **not** demonstrated; if anything this run favours the judge.
- **The comparison is confounded by independent sampling.** The two arms
  regenerate the spec and re-draw candidates separately, so they never judged the
  same programs. The execution-vs-judge difference here is dominated by sampling
  variance, not by oracle quality. A sound comparison needs both oracles to rule
  on an *identical* candidate stream (see follow-ups).
- **n=28 is far below the §5.5 target (400+).** Per-problem variance is large;
  one problem swings risk by ~0.036. Treat both numbers as point estimates with
  wide intervals.
- **Low judge η_fa is plausible for easy problems.** These problems are not hard
  to review by reading; a real judge noise floor would show more on subtle,
  concurrency-heavy, or specification-ambiguous tasks.

## Follow-ups this surfaced

1. **Shared candidate stream for `--compare`.** Both oracles should rule on the
   same generated candidates so the comparison is paired, not two independent
   samples. This is the single biggest threat to the comparison's validity.
2. **Larger, harder benchmark.** Move toward the §5.5 target and weight subtle
   semantic and concurrency defects, where a reading-only judge should struggle.
3. **Records were not saved for this run** (the `--compare` `-records` support
   landed in #6 *after* this run's binary was built). Re-run with `-records` to
   make the next pilot replayable offline.
